Why northern Peru? Historical climate-health context

El Nino is historical context for this project. No 2027 climate or epidemic forecast is generated or validated here. Consult official authorities for current forecasts.

What NinoSalud can support: a transparent review of historical surveillance, climatic anomalies, source discrepancies and district exposure classifications. It cannot generate certified outbreak warnings or predict 2027 case counts.

Official sources for current context: ENFEN announcements · MINSA CDC Peru · IMARPE coastal El Nino index (ICEN). Official outlooks are time-sensitive.

Interpretation safeguards

Not a forecast: the comparison uses the observed count from the selected week. It cannot predict that week in advance.

Not an outbreak label: no independently adjudicated outbreak events are available for confirmation. Precision, recall and lead time cannot be claimed.

Unstable reference: only five historical years, including COVID-19, define each seasonal reference; the official MINSA endemic channel excludes 2020–2022, so this exploratory threshold is NOT comparable; the reporting system can change. These are raw episode counts, not population-standardized incidence.

Scope: Piura's eight provinces only. Tumbes, Lambayeque and La Libertad have not been added to this module.

Transparent Python implementation in research/eda_reference_signals.py; download all province-week comparisons. Further database opportunities and limitations in docs/NORTHERN_PERU_SOURCE_RESEARCH.md.

Regional dengue · how long from symptom onset to recorded attendance?

A new independent audit of the GORE Piura regional dengue dataset (2023, 2025 and partial 2026), with source dictionary cross-checks. This measures the interval between the two recorded dates, not confirmed disease incidence, central reporting lag, treatment waiting time or forecast effectiveness.

Critical source mismatch: the dataset describes confirmed + possible cases without a diagnostic-status column. The 2023 snapshot ends in May, there is no 2024, and the 2026 snapshot ends June 30. It must not be merged with national confirmed-only totals.

Two distinct retrospective studies — do not confuse them

Dengue model: historical Piura-wide notifications, trained on 2018–2022 and tested on 2023–2024; displayed in tab 02. EDA model: provincial all-age episode counts, trained on 2019–2022 and tested on 2023–2025; displayed in tab 04. Both are exploratory and neither has demonstrated generalizable operational early warning. The 2026 exploratory ablation compares a matched EDA-only Random Forest against the same algorithm with observed weather features (tab 04).

EDA 2025 includes missing observed climate weeks. The EDA model evaluation excludes observations requiring those inputs, so its test metrics do not cover every week.

Critical limitations

Incomplete surveillance: a district without a dengue record is not automatically a district with zero infections.

Future information leakage: 2026 CENEPRED and RENIPRESS attributes cannot be used as if observed in 2017 or 2023.

Reporting heterogeneity: individual dengue notifications and aggregate EDA episodes have different measurement units. In particular, 2024 EDA age-5+ entries in the national export are zero from week 16 onward and conflict with the CDC Peru dashboard annual Excel (57,363 episodes in 2024); these values are marked incomplete instead of plotted. This annual total has not been used to fill the weekly series.

Geographic scale: district and departmental measurements may not generalize to neighborhoods or individual patients.

El Niño 2027: a scenario is not a verified health forecast; ENSO conditions may change.
